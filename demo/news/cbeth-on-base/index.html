The mechanism behind the growth

These cbETH supply contributions primarily come from automated, leveraged looping strategies. In simple terms: a vault supplies cbETH as collateral, borrows ETH against it, converts that ETH back into cbETH on a DEX, and supplies it again, repeating the cycle up to a fixed leverage ceiling.

01Supply cbETH as collateral
02Borrow ETH against it
03Swap the ETH to cbETH on a DEX
04Supply it again

Repeated up to a fixed leverage ceiling.

These strategies run within transparent boundaries. The leverage ceiling, the markets a vault may reach, and the actions it may take are all set onchain and visible to every participant before capital enters. The boundaries hold as they are enforced at the contract level.

Base ecosystem impact

Fusion's footprint is clearest in the Aave Base markets these strategies use, and the relationship runs both ways.

~68%of all cbETH supplied to Aave's Base cbETH market comes from vaults built on Fusion.
~38%of the WETH borrowed from Aave's Base WETH market, demand that accrues to WETH lenders as yield.

That demand has marked the market itself. As the positions grew, Aave raised the cbETH supply cap on its Base market to accommodate them.

The Fusion ecosystem's share of cbETH has grown alongside it, from roughly 11.72% of the circulating supply on 23 June to 19.3% on 15 July.

Coinbase Inc impact

cbETH is Coinbase's liquid staking token. Its value grows as Ethereum staking rewards accrue, and Coinbase takes a 25% commission on those rewards. Every cbETH the Fusion ecosystem holds is staked ETH earning that yield, so demand at this scale supports Coinbase's staking business directly.
